Thank you for contributing to panoma. This agreement exists so that the project can
accept your work and keep offering it to everyone under the AGPL-3.0, while remaining
able to sustain its development commercially. It follows the pattern used by most
dual-licensed open-source projects — the same structure Element adopted for Synapse and
Canonical has used for Ubuntu for over a decade.

You keep the copyright to everything you write. This is a licence, not a transfer.

And it binds in both directions: section 4 is the Owner's obligation to you — everything
you contribute stays available under its free licence, as a term of this contract, with
consequences if that is ever broken. Not a blog post. A clause.

Please read it before you sign. If anything is unclear, open a discussion and ask —
a signature you did not understand is worth nothing to either of us.

## 4. The Owner's obligations to You

This section is what You get back. It is a binding contractual obligation —
deliberately an obligation, not a condition on the licences in sections 2 and 3. The
difference is what happens if it ever breaks: a broken condition would turn licences

### 4.1 The free-software commitment

Whenever the Owner exercises any right granted in section 2 — sublicensing included —
the Owner agrees to also license Your Contribution, and to keep it publicly available,
under the Submission-Date Licence. Publishing Your Contribution under additional
licences — commercial ones included — never suspends or replaces this obligation: the

One exception, because the law can force it: removing a Contribution — or the affected
part of one — to comply with a legal obligation or a credible third-party infringement
claim is not a breach of this section, provided the Owner removes no more than is
required and restores what may be restored once the claim is resolved.

In plain words: the Owner may sell exceptions to the AGPL. The Owner may not withdraw
from the commons what You gave to it.

### 4.2 What happens if that is broken

If the Owner materially breaches section 4.1 with respect to a Contribution and does
not cure the breach within three months of Your written notice, You may terminate the
licences You granted in sections 2 and 3 for that Contribution, effective for the
future.

Termination is prospective only. It does not affect: (a) the rights the public has
already received under the Submission-Date Licence, which are irrevocable under that
licence's own terms; (b) sublicences of that Contribution granted to specific third
parties before termination; and (c) the Owner's continued operation of products and
services that already incorporated that Contribution when termination took effect.
What termination ends is the Owner's ability to grant new licences of Your
Contribution, and to build new products on it.

The consequence is deliberate: if the promise is ever broken, the community keeps
everything it was ever given — the legal basis for a fork is intact by design — and the
breach buys the Owner nothing further.

## How to sign

Open your pull request, then post this comment on it, exactly:

```
I have read the CLA Document and I hereby sign the CLA
```

The signature is recorded in [`.github/cla-signers.json`](.github/cla-signers.json),
in this repository and in the open — no external service holds it, and you can read who
has signed at any time. You sign once; it covers the contributions you had already
submitted when you signed and every one you submit after.

If you contribute on behalf of a company, or your employer owns your work and has not
given you written permission or waived those rights — section 5.2 covers exactly the
case where it has — **this is not the agreement you can sign**: the first statement in
section 5 asks you to say the work is your own, and in that case it isn't. Use the
[Corporate CLA](CCLA.md) instead: it is
signed by someone with authority to bind the company, and it carries a list of the people
authorised to contribute, so those people never sign anything themselves. Write to
`support@panoma.ai` from a company address before opening the pull request.
