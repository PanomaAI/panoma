# Code of Conduct

[Leer en español](translations/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.es.md)

## In one sentence

This project is built by discussing the code, not the person who wrote it.

## What we expect

Panoma is a small, technical project. Most conversations here involve disagreement about
engineering decisions, and that is healthy: a well-supported disagreement is the cheapest
way to find a bug. The disagreement must remain about the work.

- **Critique the code, not the person.** "This query opens the database without closing it"
  is a review. "You do not know what you are doing" is not, and it does not identify the
  defect either.
- **Assume good intent.** Someone who opens an incomplete issue is usually trying to help
  with the time they had. Ask for what is missing; do not assume carelessness.
- **Explain why.** This is already the rule for code comments and it applies equally to
  review. A "no" without a reason cannot be challenged or learned from.
- **Accept that the answer is sometimes no.** Panoma has a deliberately narrow purpose.
  Rejecting a good idea that does not belong in this project is not a judgment about the
  person who brought it.
- **Write in the language you know best.** English is the project's canonical language, but
  issues and review comments in Spanish are equally welcome. Nobody will correct your
  language instead of answering you.

## What we do not accept

- Insults, contempt, or personal attacks, including those wrapped in irony.
- Derogatory comments about age, disability, appearance, ethnicity, gender identity or
  expression, experience level, nationality, race, religion, or sexual orientation.
- Sexualized language or imagery, including sexual insinuation.
- Public or private harassment, including following someone from thread to thread to keep
  pressing them.
- Publishing another person's private information—physical address, email address, or
  anything they have not made public—without explicit permission.
- Any other conduct that would be considered unacceptable in a professional setting.

## Where this applies

This code applies everywhere the project exists: issues, pull requests, reviews, commits,
the repository, and any space where someone publicly represents panoma.

## Reporting a violation

Email **support@panoma.ai**. Reports are read and answered. What you share is handled
privately and is not disclosed to the reported person without speaking with you first.

Describe what happened, where it happened, and include links when available. You do not need
to decide whether it "counts" as a violation before reporting it; that is what the report is
for.

## What happens next

The maintainer investigates and chooses a response based on the circumstances:

1. **Private warning.** The conduct and its impact are explained. This is the usual response
   to a first incident that looks like a bad day rather than deliberate harm.
2. **Public warning** in the thread where the harm was public.
3. **Temporary removal** from project interaction, with a stated return date.
4. **Permanent removal** for sustained harassment or attacks on a person or group.

The maintainer makes these decisions. If you believe a decision was unfair, reply by email
and it will be reviewed.

## Origin

This policy is adapted from the
[Contributor Covenant 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
used by projects including Kubernetes and Rails. It has been rewritten in this project's
voice, but its protections and enforcement model intentionally remain the same: this is not
a document where originality is useful.
